# 2025 MTS ROV Photo Challenge

The **2025 MTS ROV Photo Challenge** is a useful visual design-reference source for the ChartRoom ROV project. The Marine Technology Society (MTS) ROV Committee describes the challenge as a way of showcasing real-world ROV work and the current state of ROV technology across the ROV community. The 2025 Flickr collection contains **128 photographs**. [MTS ROV Committee](https://www.mtsociety.org/remotely-operated-vehicles) · [2025 MTS ROV Photo Challenge](https://www.flickr.com/photos/matecenter/albums/72177720330848375/)

This source should be treated as **design inspiration and comparative research**, not as a validated design specification.

## What to look for

The photographs provide a useful opportunity to compare different approaches to:

- **Frame architecture** — open frames, modular members, bracing, equipment mounting, and protection.
- **Thruster arrangement** — propulsion geometry, separation, guarding, and accessibility.
- **Buoyancy and ballast** — flotation placement, mass distribution, and the resulting vehicle attitude.
- **Camera placement** — forward, downward, task-facing, and protected camera positions.
- **Lighting** — light placement relative to cameras, frame members, and the working envelope.
- **Payload and tooling** — manipulators, tool mounts, interchangeable payloads, and access to the work area.
- **Tether management** — strain relief, routing, and keeping the tether clear of propulsion and tooling.
- **Electronics packaging** — enclosure placement, cable routing, connector access, and serviceability.
- **Mechanical protection** — the trade-off between protecting equipment and retaining clear access around the vehicle.
- **Human factors** — transport, handling, assembly, maintenance, and modification of the vehicle.

## Relevance to the ChartRoom ROV

The value of this collection is that it contains many different vehicles rather than a single designer's solution. It can therefore be used as a **design-space survey**: recurring mechanical and systems solutions can be identified before deciding whether any are appropriate for the ChartRoom vehicle.

Particular areas worth comparing against the existing ROV architecture are:

1. frame dimensions and structural layout;
2. horizontal and vertical thruster placement;
3. buoyancy location relative to the centre of mass;
4. camera and lighting geometry;
5. protection of thrusters and cameras without unnecessarily obstructing the working envelope;
6. payload/tool mounting;
7. tether entry and strain relief; and
8. access to electronics and other service items.

## Suggested engineering use

When reviewing individual photographs, record observations as:

**Observed design → apparent purpose → possible benefit → possible disadvantage → relevance to our ROV → candidate implementation.**

This keeps the collection as engineering research rather than allowing visual inspiration to become an undocumented design decision.
