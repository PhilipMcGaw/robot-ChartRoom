# Stratospheric weather-balloon camera experiment — NateintheWild / Light Side Up

## Source

- Threads: https://www.threads.com/share/BASpfsLTxo/
- Account: **NateintheWild** (Nate Luebbe)
- Captured from a Threads post viewed on 23 September 2026.
- The supplied screenshot shows this as part **1/5** of a thread and states that the post was made approximately six years earlier.

## Summary

Nate Luebbe describes an attempt to launch a **Sony α7S III** into the stratosphere beneath a weather balloon in order to film the **aurora borealis (northern lights)** from near the edge of space.

The project involved designing and building a protective enclosure for the camera and preparing a balloon payload. The first launch attempt failed and the camera was permanently lost.

The Threads post is a retrospective account of that failed attempt. The accompanying images show:

1. A large weather balloon and the team preparing the launch at night.
2. A white payload enclosure resting on a ground sheet after the launch attempt.

The thread continues beyond the supplied screenshot; only part 1/5 is presently recorded here.

## Engineering relevance

This is useful as a case study in **high-altitude balloon payload engineering**, particularly:

- lightweight protective payload enclosures;
- operation of camera electronics in a low-temperature, low-pressure environment;
- power management for long-duration airborne payloads;
- GNSS/GPS reliability and recovery planning;
- parachute and descent-system design;
- telemetry and payload tracking;
- environmental qualification before launch;
- launch-site meteorology and wind modelling;
- recovery of an autonomous payload after balloon burst;
- regulatory and aviation constraints associated with high-altitude balloon launches.

The failure is particularly useful from an engineering perspective because the project demonstrates that a technically successful payload is not sufficient: **tracking, recovery, environmental assumptions and launch operations are part of the system design**.

## Corroborating sources

Sony Alpha Universe documented the project in December 2020. It identifies the team as **Lost Horizon Creative**, comprising Nate Luebbe (@nateinthewild), Autumn Schrock (@autpops) and Austin Smith (@austincamsmith), and describes their use of a Sony Alpha α7S III on a weather balloon to capture the northern lights. The resulting film was titled *Light Side Up*. 

- Sony Alpha Universe: [Capturing An Aurora From Above!](https://alphauniverse.com/stories/sending-a-sony-a7s-iii-into-the-stratosphere-see-how-this-team-creatively-captured-an-aurora-from-above/)
- Night Photo Summit: [Light Side Up](https://2021.nightphotosummit.com/talks/light-side-up/)

A 2020 podcast episode with Nate Luebbe describes the experiment as sending an α7S III more than **120,000 feet** on a helium-filled weather balloon to capture 4K aurora footage. It also states that the project required engineering research, meteorological considerations and attention to FAA regulations. The episode notes that the first payload was lost and asks listeners to contact Nate if they found the lost payload.

- Wild & Exposed: [Light Side Up with Nate Luebbe](https://www.wildandexposed.com/journal/2020/9/29/we-podcast-episode54)

Sony's later account describes the completed project as a lesson in persistence and confirms that the team ultimately recovered footage after sending the camera into the stratosphere.
