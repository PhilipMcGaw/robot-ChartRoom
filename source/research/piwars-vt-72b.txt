===== Why it is interesting =====

The VT-72B is an armoured recovery vehicle rather than a conventional tank. The full-size vehicle is based on the T-72 family and is characterised by recovery equipment including a front dozer blade, winch, and crane. Independent references confirm the vehicle's recovery role and tracked configuration. source reference unavailable (turn2search0, turn2search24)

For a PiWars-style robot, the recovery-vehicle concept is potentially more interesting than simply reproducing a tank. It suggests a vehicle built around useful mechanical functions rather than a weapon/turret theme:

  * tracked mobility;
  * a front blade/plough or pushing attachment;
  * a lifting or articulated mechanism;
  * a winch or towing mechanism;
  * a compact serviceable chassis; and
  * multiple competition tasks using the same base vehicle.

===== PiWars concept =====

Consider a small tracked competition robot inspired by the VT-72B recovery-vehicle layout, but designed specifically around PiWars challenges.

Potential features:

  - **Tracked chassis** — prioritise traction, turning performance, ground clearance, and robustness.
  - **Front blade** — use as a pushing, clearing, or ball-handling attachment where competition rules permit.
  - **Articulated arm/crane** — investigate a lightweight servo/gear-driven mechanism for lifting or manipulating objects.
  - **Winch** — potentially useful for pulling or recovery challenges, subject to the rules.
  - **Modular front tool interface** — make the blade, arm, or other mechanisms replaceable rather than designing one fixed-purpose machine.
  - **Camera/telemetry** — potentially reuse the Robots/Cockpit architecture where that provides a useful development or testing environment.

===== Engineering questions =====

Before turning this into a build proposal, investigate:

  * whether the CactusCraft model is a usable mechanical starting point or only visual inspiration;
  * whether its dimensions and construction are suitable for an RC/PiWars conversion;
  * track width, sprocket geometry, suspension, and available motor volume;
  * battery and motor-driver packaging;
  * whether a front blade can be made structurally useful without making the robot excessively heavy;
  * mechanism limits and safe-state behaviour for an articulated arm or winch;
  * PiWars rules and event-specific restrictions on mechanisms;
  * whether the chassis can accept interchangeable tools; and
  * whether a tracked recovery-vehicle theme gives a genuine competition advantage over a simpler differential-drive platform.
