# K-9 prop and storyline tracker

## Purpose

This page tracks two related but distinct subjects:

1. K-9's narrative history: the stories, eras, operators, locations, capabilities, repairs, upgrades, and continuity claims.
2. K-9's physical representation: screen-used props, rebuilt or modified props, stunt units, close-ups, replicas, toys, publicity models, and later production designs.

The purpose is to compare the prop or model shown on screen with the storyline context in which it appears. A visual difference must not automatically be treated as a narrative redesign, and a narrative statement must not automatically be treated as evidence about the physical prop.

## Evidence rules

Classify each observation as one of the following:

- **Screen evidence** — visible in an episode, film, trailer, production photograph, or documented behind-the-scenes material.
- **Production evidence** — a prop, model, workshop photograph, production document, interview, or contemporary production account.
- **Licensed product evidence** — toy, kit, model, packaging, or catalogue material. This can document how K-9 was marketed, but does not prove the on-screen design.
- **Later recollection** — an interview or recollection made after production.
- **Inference** — a proposed explanation for a difference that still needs evidence.
- **Unverified claim** — a statement recorded for later checking.

Keep screen continuity, production history, licensed products, and fan or later recollection separate.

## Comparison questions

For each appearance, ask:

- Is the same physical unit visible, or could this be a replacement, stunt unit, close-up unit, or rebuild?
- Does the storyline mention damage, repair, upgrade, reconstruction, disguise, or a change of body?
- Are differences limited to finish, lighting, aerials, tread details, head shape, or control-panel layout?
- Is the image from the actual story, a publicity shoot, a prop photograph, a replica, or a licensed product?
- Does the apparent change occur between stories, during a story, or only between camera angles?
- Is there a production reason documented for the change?
- Does the proposed explanation require an unsupported continuity claim?

## Pasted reference: prop construction and continuity claims

The following summary is derived from user-supplied pasted text. The original publication, author, and source date were not included, so these details are recorded as secondary research claims pending verification.

### Character and designation

The text describes K-9, K-9, or K•9 as a designation used for a series of intelligent, dog-like robots associated with Professor Marius, the Fourth Doctor, Leela, Romana, Sarah Jane Smith, Luke Smith, and Starkey. It identifies four versions: Mark I, Mark II, Mark III, and Mark IV.

It also records recurring character conventions:

- addressing a designated owner as “Master” or “Mistress”;
- using “affirmative” and “negative”;
- literal interpretation of instructions;
- a loyal and logical personality; and
- striped collars echoing the Fourth Doctor's scarf.

These are storyline and character-history claims, separate from evidence about the physical props.

### Original design and fabrication

The pasted account says that the script described K-9 as a personal “Komputa” and that Bob Baker and Dave Martin initially imagined the role as a performer in a dog-shaped suit. Ian Scoones produced preliminary sketches, after which Tony Harding took responsibility for the final design and construction under a short schedule.

The account says Harding's brief was to ensure that the machine did not appear to contain a person, leading to a remote-controlled construction. It describes the design as developing from an early form-test shell into a larger-headed machine once the required mechanisms were understood.

Reported mechanisms include:

- rotating ear probes;
- a telescopic eye probe;
- an extendible computer and “Doctor Who nose” assembly;
- flashing lights on the top and eye panel;
- moving tail antennae;
- a ticker-tape tongue;
- vertical head movement; and
- a radio-control system.

The reported original finish was gold/grey metallic with a tartan collar and a silver disc. The text claims that the disc was blank rather than engraved.

### Prop mechanics and operation

The pasted account describes a chassis containing batteries, servos, and a motor, with one steerable front wheel connected by chain drive. It reports that the radio-control system could interfere with studio cameras, producing delayed recordings and unintended movement.

It also describes the chain drive as extremely noisy, requiring loud incidental music to conceal the motor sound in some scenes. These claims are useful because they connect visible performance limitations with the physical construction of the prop, but they require production-source verification.

### Rebuilds and prop units

The text presents a distinction between television continuity and physical production history:

- Mark I was written out in *The Invasion of Time*, remaining on Gallifrey with Leela.
- The story provided an in-universe explanation for a replacement by showing the Doctor producing a box of components for a new K-9.
- The replacement retained the shell while receiving changed internal mechanics.
- A rubber belt replaced the original chain drive, although the belt reportedly failed during use.
- A lightweight duplicate shell was made for scenes where actors needed to carry K-9.
- The stronger motor of the later version still required hidden tracks or strings during some location filming.

The text also reports a later overhaul before Season 18, including stripped and replaced internal mechanics, larger wheels, front- and rear-wheel drive, and more reliable radio control. These details should be mapped against episode dates, production photographs, and prop-maker or effects-department accounts.

### Colour and appearance ledger

| Period or production | Claimed finish or change | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| *The Invisible Enemy* to *The Invasion of Time* | Gold/grey metallic finish; tartan collar and silver disc | Pasted secondary claim |
| *The Ribos Operation* to *Warriors' Gate* | Darker grey finish | Pasted secondary claim |
| *K9 and Company — A Girl's Best Friend* onwards | Blue/grey finish and a carrying handle | Pasted secondary claim |

The pasted text states that the prop had three principal colour schemes. These should be checked against episode footage and production stills, since lighting, repainting, duplicate shells, and restoration may affect apparent colour.

### Storyline versus prop history

The text proposes that K-9's physical rebuilds were partly explained in continuity, while the underlying production reality involved one prop being repainted and rebuilt several times, plus lightweight duplicates. This is directly relevant to the tracker: a narrative Mark number, a screen appearance, and a physical prop unit should be recorded as separate fields.

The reported storyline changes include:

- a move from Marius's 5000 AD technology to the Doctor's technology;
- a gun limited to stunning rather than killing;
- response to a dog whistle; and
- a memory bank combining K-9, the Doctor, and TARDIS knowledge.

These are narrative capability claims and should not be used as evidence of changes to the physical electronics without production documentation.

### Voice

The pasted text identifies John Leeson as K-9's original voice performer. It claims that a voice modulator was used for *The Invisible Enemy*, with later distortion supplied by Leeson's performance. This belongs in the storyline and production register rather than the physical-prop register.

## Pasted K-9 timeline: appearance and prop-history claims

Primary Chartroom page: [K-9 timeline](../projects/k9-timeline.md). It was migrated from the [K9 Timeline — PhilipMcGaw.com](https://philipmcgaw.com/projects/k-9/k9-timeline/) page, which remains recorded as migration provenance until the old page is removed.

This section summarises a second user-supplied timeline post. The original author, publication date, and image sources were not included in the pasted material. It is therefore recorded as a secondary visual-reference source, not as independently verified continuity.

### Mark I — Marius and the Fourth Doctor

The source associates the first K-9 with Professor Frederick Marius, who created K-9 in the year 5000 while working on asteroid K4067. It describes Marius's K-9 as incorporating a medical computer, an advanced responsive brain, anti-radiation protection, probes, a laser scalpel, vision and voice systems, and scanning antennas.

The source says Mark I remained with Leela on Gallifrey after *The Invasion of Time*. It visually describes the early screen prop as light grey-silver with a black rear control panel.

Listed appearances include:

- *The Invisible Enemy*
- *Image of the Fendahl*
- *The Sun Makers*
- *Underworld*
- *The Invasion of Time*

The source also lists appearances in the 2009 *K9* series, but those entries require separate verification because the production and prop relationship are not established by the pasted material.

### Mark II — darker finish and rear-panel changes

The source describes Mark II as the same basic prop with a darker gunmetal-grey finish. It states that the rear control panel matched the body colour and sat lower in the shell at some points, later appearing raised by *Warriors' Gate*.

It makes an important practical observation: the rear control panel was removable for transport, so its height may vary between filming sessions. This is a useful prop-continuity caution because an apparent structural change may instead be a setup or transport variation.

The source associates Mark II with:

- Season 16: *The Ribos Operation*, *The Pirate Planet*, *The Stones of Blood*, *The Androids of Tara*, and *The Armageddon Factor*;
- Season 17: *Destiny of the Daleks*, *The Creature from the Pit*, *Nightmare of Eden*, *The Horns of Nimon*, and *Shada*;
- Season 18: *The Leisure Hive*, *Meglos*, *Full Circle*, *State of Decay*, and *Warriors' Gate*; and
- the BBC Christmas Tape and BBCi *Shada* material.

The Mark I/Mark II boundary should be cross-checked against production dates and the rebuild account already recorded above. It should not be assumed that every listed appearance used a unique physical prop.

### Mark III — K-9 and Company through School Reunion

The source places Mark III with the Fourth Doctor and Adric briefly before K-9 was given to Sarah Jane Smith. It describes the *K9 and Company — A Girl's Best Friend* appearance as the same basic prop repainted with a slight blue tint, with apparently metallic-blue neck tubing.

It lists later appearances in:

- *The Five Doctors*;
- *School Reunion*; and
- *Search Out Science*.

For *Search Out Science*, the source reports a possible green tint, redesigned ears with ribs, and the removal of three inner ear loops. It explicitly notes that the colour may be a filming effect, which should be retained as an uncertainty rather than treated as a confirmed repaint.

For *School Reunion*, it describes the original dummy prop as refitted with a new head and working parts, painted rust-spotted grey-blue, with the ear loops restored. This claim is particularly useful for separating dummy, hero, and rebuilt units, but needs production evidence.

### Mark IV — Sarah Jane Adventures and later rebuilds

The source describes Mark IV as a return to the original 1977 hero prop, now visibly older and more worn, retaining the later ear design and a brighter version of the *K9 and Company* blue finish.

It lists appearances in:

- *School Reunion*;
- *Journey's End*;
- *The Sarah Jane Adventures* series 1 and 3; and
- *The Sarah Jane Adventures* series 4.

The source says that the original prop received a substantial electrical and drive-system overhaul by Series 3 of *The Sarah Jane Adventures*. It also notes changing identification tags, including a metal disc and a later dog-shaped tag, and a third handle on the rear in later museum/display photographs.

The precise boundary between Mark III and Mark IV appears to be a fan or production-history classification rather than a simple new-build chronology. The tracker should record the label, visual state, physical unit, and storyline identity separately.

### Replica and museum-display evidence

The source distinguishes at least two non-screen display contexts:

- an Andrew Beech replica photographed at the Doctor Who exhibition in Cardiff; and
- a Mat Irvine K-9 displayed at the National Film and Sci-Fi Museum in Milton Keynes, described as configured for later *Sarah Jane Adventures* and television appearances.

These should be kept in the prop register as display/replica evidence and must not be used as direct evidence for the screen appearance of a particular episode.

The source states that Mat Irvine had been K-9's sole operator since around 1990, and records a museum visit dated 2024-11-02. Both claims require independent attribution before being treated as established production history.

## Further research leads

These sources are stronger than a general fan-wiki summary and are worth following for the prop/storyline comparison.

## Early design evidence

The supplied image is identified as Tony Harding's original K-9 sketch. It is useful as early concept and production-design evidence, but it should not be treated as a drawing of the final screen-used prop without a supporting source.

The sketch appears to show several early design ideas:

- a low, wedge-shaped body;
- tracked running gear;
- a raised head and neck assembly;
- a projecting snout;
- aerials or antenna elements;
- a top-mounted feature or panel; and
- large side lettering or markings that may be design notation rather than final production text.

These details should be compared with the finished prop while allowing for the normal transition from concept sketch to fabricated screen object. In particular, the sketch can establish design intent, but it cannot by itself establish the exact materials, dimensions, colours, electronics, movement mechanisms, or continuity status of the final prop.
