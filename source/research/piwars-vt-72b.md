# PiWars Idea — 1:10 VT-72B

## Source

[CactusCraft — VT-72B 1:10](https://cactuscraft.com/collections/new/products/vt-72b-1-10)

The supplied CactusCraft product page could not be retrieved reliably during this review, so detailed product specifications have deliberately not been inferred from the link.

## Why it is interesting

The VT-72B is an armoured recovery vehicle rather than a conventional tank. The full-size vehicle is based on the T-72 family and is characterised by recovery equipment including a front dozer blade, winch, and crane. Independent references confirm the vehicle's recovery role and tracked configuration. citeturn2search0turn2search24

For a PiWars-style robot, the recovery-vehicle concept is potentially more interesting than simply reproducing a tank. It suggests a vehicle built around useful mechanical functions rather than a weapon/turret theme:

- tracked mobility;
- a front blade/plough or pushing attachment;
- a lifting or articulated mechanism;
- a winch or towing mechanism;
- a compact serviceable chassis; and
- multiple competition tasks using the same base vehicle.

## PiWars concept

Consider a small tracked competition robot inspired by the VT-72B recovery-vehicle layout, but designed specifically around PiWars challenges.

Potential features:

1. **Tracked chassis** — prioritise traction, turning performance, ground clearance, and robustness.
2. **Front blade** — use as a pushing, clearing, or ball-handling attachment where competition rules permit.
3. **Articulated arm/crane** — investigate a lightweight servo/gear-driven mechanism for lifting or manipulating objects.
4. **Winch** — potentially useful for pulling or recovery challenges, subject to the rules.
5. **Modular front tool interface** — make the blade, arm, or other mechanisms replaceable rather than designing one fixed-purpose machine.
6. **Camera/telemetry** — potentially reuse the Robots/Cockpit architecture where that provides a useful development or testing environment.

## Engineering questions

Before turning this into a build proposal, investigate:

- whether the CactusCraft model is a usable mechanical starting point or only visual inspiration;
- whether its dimensions and construction are suitable for an RC/PiWars conversion;
- track width, sprocket geometry, suspension, and available motor volume;
- battery and motor-driver packaging;
- whether a front blade can be made structurally useful without making the robot excessively heavy;
- mechanism limits and safe-state behaviour for an articulated arm or winch;
- PiWars rules and event-specific restrictions on mechanisms;
- whether the chassis can accept interchangeable tools; and
- whether a tracked recovery-vehicle theme gives a genuine competition advantage over a simpler differential-drive platform.
