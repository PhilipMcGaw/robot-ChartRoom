MATE ROV Competition — ROV Engineering Requirements Summary
===============================================================

Purpose
-------
Summary of useful engineering practices from the MATE ROV Competition for
application to the ChartRoom ROV. Competition-specific limits are deliberately
excluded; the aim is to adopt good engineering requirements rather than copy
competition rules.

Recommended requirements
-------------------------

SAFETY
- Maintain a documented ROV safety case covering electrical, mechanical,
  thermal, pressure, propulsion, tether, software, and operational hazards.
- Maintain pre-deployment, operation, and recovery checklists.
- Loss of command/communications shall place the vehicle in a defined safe
  state within a specified timeout (target: <= 1 s).
- Loss of tether power shall disable propulsion within a specified timeout
  (target: <= 1 s).
- Recovery from a communications fault shall require an explicit propulsion
  re-enable.
- Exposed propulsion components shall have effective guards.
- Accessible sharp edges, uncontrolled cutting hazards, and mechanical
  pinch points shall be eliminated or protected.

MECHANICAL AND ENVIRONMENTAL
- Provide dedicated mechanical tether strain relief independent of electrical
  conductors and connectors.
- Define a maximum operating depth and design pressure for every pressure
  enclosure.
- Hydrostatically test pressure-rated enclosures before operational use.
- Record pressure-test results against enclosure identity and revision.
- Document and validate the environmental protection method for every wet
  electrical connection.
- Major subsystems should be replaceable without unnecessary disassembly of
  unrelated systems.

ELECTRICAL AND POWER
- Every connector shall be rated for its voltage, current, environment, and
  required mating cycles.
- Wiring shall have defined routing, strain relief, abrasion protection, and
  identification.
- Characterise ROV power consumption under representative maximum-load
  conditions while submerged.
- Record bus voltage, steady-state current, peak current, and major subsystem
  loads.
- Implement configurable propulsion power/current limiting.

SOFTWARE, CONTROL, AND VIDEO
- Keep propulsion safety, timeout, neutral, and E-stop functions under the
  Control subsystem rather than the pilot UI.
- Make vehicle-control software testable without physical hardware.
- Use SIL/HiL testing before physical deployment where reasonably practicable.
- Provide a primary pilot camera with adequate field of view and latency.
- Detect and clearly indicate loss of the primary pilot video feed.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
- Maintain a System Integration Diagram showing power, communications,
  control, propulsion, sensors, video, payloads, and safety interfaces.
- Keep clear ownership boundaries between Cockpit, Control, Datalogger, and
  HiL/SiL.
- Preserve modular interfaces so hardware and software can be replaced or
  simulated independently.

TEST AND VERIFICATION
- Progress testing from subsystem tests through integration tests to complete
  vehicle tests.
- Retain test results against the relevant hardware and software revision.
- Use measured data rather than nominal component values where practical,
  particularly for propulsion and power budgets.

ENGINEERING DOCUMENTATION
- Record significant design decisions, including requirements, alternatives,
  selection criteria, evidence, decision, and consequences.
- Maintain documentation so that a competent engineer unfamiliar with the
  project can understand, operate, maintain, and modify the ROV.
- Treat the engineering knowledge base as controlled project documentation,
  not merely as a collection of build notes.

Most valuable MATE-derived additions for ChartRoom
---------------------------------------------------
1. Formal safety case and operational checklists.
2. Explicit communications/power-loss safe-state requirements.
3. Proper mechanical tether strain relief.
4. Defined depth ratings and hydrostatic testing of pressure housings.
5. Measured submerged full-load power/current testing.
6. Configurable propulsion power limiting.
7. Formal System Integration Diagram.
8. SIL/HiL as part of the normal control-software verification process.
9. Design-decision records.
10. Revision-controlled test evidence.

What NOT to copy
----------------
MATE competition-specific constraints such as competition supply limits,
mandatory connectors/fuses, vehicle mass limits, competition depth limits,
specific tether dimensions, crew arrangements, or documentation page limits
should not become ChartRoom requirements unless they serve an independent
design purpose.

Overall conclusion
------------------
The most useful lesson from MATE is that the ROV should be treated as a
systems-engineering project. The existing ChartRoom architecture of Cockpit,
Control, Datalogger, NATS communications, and HiL/SiL is already well aligned
with this approach. The main gap is to make safety, verification, measured
performance, and engineering documentation explicit requirements.
