===== Summary of the source =====

Propeller Pages explains that a close-fitting duct can reduce flow around propeller blade tips, and describes nozzle designs intended to produce useful thrust at low vessel speed. It contrasts the Kort 19A and Rice-Speed profiles and states that nozzle drag can reduce the benefit as forward speed increases. The page gives comparative drag coefficients and claims that the nozzle itself can contribute a substantial part of total thrust in the Rice-Speed example.

These are claims and explanations from the source page. It does not provide a test method, operating conditions, uncertainty, or a traceable technical report for the numerical comparisons on the page. The figures and simplified flow explanation should therefore be treated as unverified source claims, not as general design values.

===== Relevance and limitations =====

This page is a starting point for comparing open propellers and ducted propellers, particularly where low-speed thrust and physical propeller guarding matter. It also highlights a design trade-off: a nozzle's contribution depends on its profile and operating condition, and drag at higher advance speed can reduce or remove a thrust advantage.

The page concerns marine propulsion generally; it does not test a small remotely operated vehicle (ROV) thruster, specify a suitable nozzle for a project motor, or establish thrust, efficiency, handling, or safety for any particular build. Do not use its numerical claims as a design basis without an appropriate primary technical source and measurements for the intended propeller, duct, motor, and operating conditions.
