===== Project overview =====

The [[https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/self-sufficient-mobile-weather-station/|Raspberry Pi article “Self-sufficient mobile weather station”]] describes a portable weather station built around a Raspberry Pi Zero W. The creator reports using a solar panel and a modular kit that can be assembled for measurement and taken apart for transport. The article identifies temperature and humidity sensing, a Hall-effect anemometer for wind speed, and light measurement using the solar panel. It also reports that the station established a web server and stored sampled data during a field test on the Gower Peninsula in Wales.

The article says the enclosure was iterated to achieve a reported IP65 rating, with gaskets and cable grommets sealing the electronics. This is the creator's description of the design target; no independent ingress-protection test report is provided in the article.

===== Reported field findings =====

The creator reports that the anemometer bearings stalled at slower wind speeds because friction prevented rotation. Lubricating the bearings improved its behaviour. The temperature sensor consistently read approximately 1–2 °C higher than expected; the creator attributed this to heat trapped in the sensor head, possibly including self-heating, with insufficient heat escape. These are reported observations from the project's field test, not independent measurements or a complete calibration study.

The article does not give a quantified battery or solar-energy budget, operating duration through cloudy conditions, detailed sensor models, or long-term deployment data. The creator lists easier sampling-rate adjustment, improved modularity, and correction of the temperature issue as future improvements. Do not treat these as completed changes.

===== Relevance to weather station and buoy work =====

  * **Portable weather station:** the removable modules and transport-focused packaging may inform a field kit that can be deployed at different sites. The reported sensor-heating problem makes thermal separation and ventilation important topics for any compact enclosure.
  * **Self-powered sensing:** solar charging and local data storage are relevant patterns to compare with the [[sites:chartroom:research:solar-powered-wifi-weather-station-v40|Solar Powered WiFi Weather Station V4.0]], [[sites:chartroom:research:printables-weather-station-one-central-station|Weather Station One]], and the [[sites:chartroom:research:pimoroni-enviro-weather|Pimoroni Enviro Weather station kit]]. The article does not supply enough power data to size a system.
  * **Smart buoy:** modular payload packaging, solar power, environmental measurements, and logging are useful comparison points for the [[sites:chartroom:research:open-source-ocean-buoy-references|open-source ocean buoy references]]. The reported station is a land-based weather instrument; it provides no evidence of buoyancy design, saltwater corrosion resistance, water ingress performance in marine service, or reliable radio telemetry from a buoy.
  * **Wind measurement:** the low-speed bearing stall is a concrete reminder to examine starting threshold, friction, and calibration in the chosen anemometer rather than assuming rotation begins at all useful wind speeds.

This is a third-party project reference. It is not an adopted design, a validated weather instrument, or evidence of a tested Smart Buoy subsystem.
