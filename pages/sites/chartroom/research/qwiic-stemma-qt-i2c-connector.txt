===== Source and scope =====

**Primary discussion:** [[https://hackaday.com/2026/11/12/just-use-qwiic-already/|Hackaday — Just Use QWIIC Already]], Arya Voronova, 2026-11-12.

The article's central recommendation is to standardise the physical connector used to expose I²C buses on custom boards, rather than requiring every project to adopt a particular branded sensor range. The author describes using Qwiic connections for modular peripheral wiring, board bring-up, and direct logic-analyser access to the bus.

This note records the connector/interface concept and its potential relevance to the Robots projects. It does not record a completed design decision, selected component, or implemented hardware change.

===== Interface summary =====

Qwiic is SparkFun's 3.3 V I²C interconnect system using a keyed four-pin JST-SH connector with 1.0 mm pitch. Adafruit STEMMA QT uses the same JST-SH connector and pin order, so the two connector ecosystems are mechanically compatible. Electrical compatibility still requires checking the supply and logic specifications of the particular controller and peripheral: Qwiic is a 3.3 V system, while some STEMMA QT devices include their own regulation or level shifting and accept a wider supply range.

The standard Qwiic cable colour convention is:

| Pin | Signal | Cable colour |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GND | Black |
| 2 | 3.3 V | Red |
| 3 | SDA | Blue |
| 4 | SCL | Yellow |

The connector exposes power and the two I²C signals only. It does not include an interrupt line, reset, or general-purpose I/O. Devices that require an interrupt should use a separately routed host GPIO or another suitable interface.

SparkFun gives a conservative maximum current of 226 mA for a Qwiic cable. Treat this as a low-power peripheral connection, not a supply for motors, servos, or other substantial loads. Check the current budget of the host board, cable, connector, and each attached peripheral.

===== Engineering value =====

The principal benefit is a repeatable, detachable interface, not a change to the I²C protocol:

  * **Peripheral modularity:** compatible sensors and small modules can be connected or replaced without soldering.
  * **Board bring-up:** a standard connector provides a convenient point to scan, exercise, and verify devices before integrating them into the complete firmware.
  * **Debug access:** the exposed SDA, SCL, and ground provide a convenient logic-analyser connection for observing bus transactions and diagnosing software/device-interface faults.
  * **Reusable hardware:** common cables and adapter boards can be shared between robot controllers and peripheral breakouts.
  * **PCB design consistency:** custom boards can expose a consistent I²C expansion/debug connector, even when the connected device is not sold as a Qwiic-branded product.

The Hackaday article identifies a useful KiCad starting point: symbol `Conn_01x04_MountingPin` and horizontal JST-SH footprint `JST_SH_SM04B-SRSS-TB_1x04-1MP_P1.00mm_Horizontal`. Check the selected library version and manufacturer's recommended land pattern before using these in a released PCB. The article also warns that the vertical connector footprint variant has a different pin orientation; verify pin-to-signal mapping against the exact part and footprint rather than assuming the horizontal mapping carries over.

===== Relevance to the Robots projects =====

Potential common applications include:

  * **K9:** IMU, compass, time-of-flight distance sensors, environmental sensors, and small auxiliary displays.
  * **PiWars:** modular distance sensors, IMUs, GPIO expanders, and test/debug access on custom controller boards.
  * **SwarmBot:** reusable sensor modules and peripheral interfaces across multiple robot units, supporting common wiring and educational experimentation.

These are candidate applications, not selected components or confirmed requirements. Check each device's I²C address, voltage, current, library support, and interrupt requirements before selection. Where devices have fixed or conflicting addresses, consider address selection, a suitable I²C multiplexer, or a different interface as appropriate.

===== Design guidance =====

The user has explicitly noted awareness of I²C signal-integrity constraints. This research is about connector standardisation, modularity, and debug access; it is not an argument that Qwiic makes I²C suitable for arbitrary cable lengths, wiring topologies, or electrically noisy environments.

Proposed convention for future robot controller and peripheral PCBs:

  * Use Qwiic/STEMMA QT-compatible JST-SH connectors for suitable short-range, low-power I²C peripheral connections.
  * Expose each relevant I²C bus at an accessible, clearly labelled connector or test point so that firmware bring-up and logic-analyser measurements do not require probing fine-pitch IC pins.
  * Identify the bus/controller and supply voltage at each connector. Do not assume two physically identical connectors belong to the same I²C bus.
  * Check the exact peripheral's voltage compatibility; do not apply 5 V to a Qwiic-only device.
  * Account for aggregate pull-ups, bus capacitance, cable characteristics, topology, clock rate, and the electrical environment during the actual design review. Connector choice does not remove these requirements.
  * Route any required interrupt signal separately, or select a connector/interface that explicitly includes it.
  * Keep the Qwiic power rail within its verified current budget and provide separate power distribution for actuators.
  * Use CAN, RS-485, or another suitable link where a distributed module or longer/noisier connection needs a more robust communications interface.

This is a proposed shared design convention for consideration across K9, PiWars, and SwarmBot. It is not yet a project-wide implementation decision. No PCB, cable harness, or robot has been modified or validated as a result of this research.
