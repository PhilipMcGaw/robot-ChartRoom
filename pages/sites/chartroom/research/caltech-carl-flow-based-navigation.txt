===== Research summary =====

The 2021 paper “Learning efficient navigation in vortical flow fields” evaluates deep reinforcement learning (RL) for steering a simulated swimmer through unsteady two-dimensional flows. It reports that local velocity measurements helped the learned policy navigate the tested flow, while a policy trained on one flow did not transfer effectively to a different flow without further training. The paper's navigation results are simulation results; its discussion identifies testing transfer to real, complex, three-dimensional flows as future work.

In a 2025 follow-up, the team demonstrated a separate, simpler flow-based propulsion strategy with the Caltech Autonomous Reinforcement Learning robot (CARL) in a 13,000-litre tank. A single onboard inertial measurement unit (IMU) sensed acceleration from a passing vortex ring; a short impulsive manoeuvre positioned the robot to be carried across the tank by the vortex. The paper reports nearly fivefold lower energy use for this tank traversal than self-propelled travel over the same distance. This is a controlled laboratory result, not an ocean trial or a general vehicle performance figure. The later strategy is described as simple control logic, rather than the earlier RL navigation policy.

===== Relevance and limits =====

The work is useful background for distinguishing flow-aware autonomy from conventional navigation that treats currents only as disturbances. It also illustrates the evidence progression from simulation to a specific physical tank experiment. Neither paper establishes that the methods transfer to a tethered remotely operated vehicle, a different vehicle geometry, or unconstrained sea conditions. Chartroom has not adopted or validated these techniques.
