===== Scope =====

This note records a community recommendation seen in the DIY ROV Facebook group. It is a lead for further investigation, not a selected component, verified combination, or project specification.

===== Reported configuration =====

A group member, Steve Jacoby, reported using the following motor on his ROV:

  * **Motor:** 100 W waterproof brushless motor, model/size designation 2418, marketed for underwater robots, ROVs, and unmanned boats.
  * **Motor source:** [[https://www.underwaterthruster.com/collections/underwater-motor/products/waterproof-brushless-motor-100w-2418-corrosion-and-oxidation-resistant-for-use-in-underwater-robots-rovs-unmanned-boats-etc|Underwater Thruster — Waterproof brushless motor 100 W 2418]].
  * **ESC:** 12 A bidirectional ESC, reportedly sourced from AliExpress. The contributor did not identify a specific listing or model.
  * **Environment:** The contributor reported that the arrangement had worked in both salt water and fresh water. This is anecdotal operating experience; no duration, depth, duty cycle, test conditions, or inspection evidence was supplied.

The original post was made by Marco Paccagnella in the DIY ROV Facebook group. The screenshot supplied for this research note showed the recommendation in a comment by Steve Jacoby. A direct permalink to the Facebook post was not available in the supplied screenshot.

===== Engineering relevance =====

The combination is potentially relevant to a compact, portable ROV because it suggests a small brushless motor paired with a bidirectional ESC. The arrangement could be investigated as a lower-cost alternative to purchasing complete integrated thruster units.

Bidirectional operation is relevant to reversible thrust. However, the reported ESC rating alone does not establish compatibility, continuous current capability, thermal performance, or safe operation underwater.

===== Further investigation required =====

Before considering this combination for a prototype, establish:

  * motor operating-voltage range, winding configuration, rated and peak current, and whether the 100 W rating is electrical input or mechanical output;
  * motor thrust and torque curves with the intended propeller/nozzle, including forward/reverse asymmetry;
  * continuous and peak ESC current ratings, thermal limits, cooling requirements, and suitability for the actual supply voltage;
  * command interface, PWM frequency and pulse range, neutral calibration, arming sequence, reversal dead time, and failsafe behaviour;
  * whether the ESC is intended to be immersed, potted, oil-filled, or installed in a dry pressure enclosure;
  * cable-entry, connector, corrosion, galvanic-compatibility, and pressure/depth limits; and
  * measured power consumption and temperature during representative sustained manoeuvres.

A waterproof motor does not make the ESC, connectors, wiring, or complete assembly suitable for immersion. The supplier's claims and the community report need independent verification. A 12 A label should not be treated as a continuous rating without a datasheet and thermal test evidence.

===== Relevance to the ROV project =====

Keep this as a **research candidate only**. Compare it with complete commercial thrusters, including Blue Robotics-style units, on total installed cost, thrust per unit volume, efficiency, depth capability, serviceability, corrosion management, and integration effort.

If investigated experimentally, retain the project's separation between high-level vehicle control and low-level propulsion/safety functions. Each thruster channel should have defined neutral behaviour, command timeout, communications-loss response, and a means of inhibiting unintended thrust. The specific ESC protocol and failsafe implementation remain unknown.

No motor, ESC, or thruster architecture decision is made by this reference.
