===== Scope =====

This note records Hackaday's report on the PhotonPower Zero and the project's primary [GitHub repository](https://github.com/DavidMurrayP2P/PhotonPowerZero). The repository describes a Raspberry Pi Zero add-on board and supporting code, documentation, and 3D-printable parts for outdoor solar-powered deployments.

===== Reported design =====

The maintainer's design notes describe a power-management board built around a BQ2407 charger and an ATtiny84 microcontroller clocked at 1 MHz. The board accepts 5–9 V from USB or a solar panel, monitors the single-cell lithium-polymer battery, and controls a 5 V boost supply for the Raspberry Pi Zero when the battery has sufficient charge. The microcontroller can request a graceful Pi shutdown and send battery information to the Pi.

The repository includes code, KiCad PCB files, 3D-printable parts, user documentation, energy-consumption guidance, and solar-panel enclosure builds. The project is released under the CERN Open Hardware Licence Version 2, Strongly Reciprocal (CERN-OHL-S-2.0), according to the repository's licence file.

The README says 6 W solar panels were used during testing and that the maintainer now recommends 9–10 W panels described in the 10 W build guide. The maintainer and Hackaday report outdoor testing across Australian seasons; these are project reports, not an independent energy-yield or environmental qualification.

===== Relevance =====

This is a reference for separating low-power battery monitoring and power switching from a Raspberry Pi application. Graceful shutdown, battery telemetry, and planned energy consumption can support outdoor data loggers and environmental sensing nodes where the Pi must stop safely during low battery conditions.

===== Limitations =====

The repository says the board is designed for Raspberry Pi Zero-class power use and does not support Raspberry Pi 3, 4, or 5 or Jetson-class boards without a different design. The LiPo battery is not included and must be sourced separately. The project's solar and temperature reports do not establish suitability for a marine enclosure, saltwater exposure, or the Robots project's electronics and battery system.
