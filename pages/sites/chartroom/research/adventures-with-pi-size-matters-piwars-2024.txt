===== Source =====

  * **Author/site:** Footleg, *Adventures with a Raspberry Pi*
  * **Article:** [Size Matters](https://adventures-with-pi.blogspot.com/2024/01/size-matters.html)
  * **Published:** 20 January 2024
  * **Context:** PiWars 2024 robot design, labelled PiWars and PiWars2024 by the author.

This note records the author's account. The engineering observations below are interpretations of that account, not claims that the proposed chassis was built or validated.

===== What the source documents =====

The author was developing a tracked rocker-bogie robot concept and found that the selected motors and track assemblies could not be arranged within the PiWars maximum robot dimensions.

The options considered included:

  * Mounting motors back-to-back and using a two-stage belt drive, keeping the belts in line with the motor bodies.
  * Using 45-degree bevel gears to turn the drive through 90 degrees, allowing the motor bodies to lie along the robot's length rather than across its width.
  * Offsetting the front and rear track assemblies to make them overlap in plan view.

The more fundamental issue was the overall length. Placing two track assemblies one ahead of the other left insufficient clearance for the tracks to pivot independently in the rocker-bogie arrangement without the front tracks fouling the rear tracks. Offsetting the assemblies reduced the available width too much.

The author concluded that the concept would not fit the competition's dimensional envelope and decided to abandon it in favour of a new design.

===== Engineering observations =====

  * **Check the complete envelope early.** Width and length must be assessed together; solving a motor-width problem does not necessarily solve the overall packaging problem.
  * **Include articulation and swept volume.** A rocker-bogie layout must be checked throughout its permitted movement, not only in a static CAD pose. Track-to-track and track-to-chassis interference are important constraints.
  * **Treat transmission architecture as a packaging variable.** Belt stages and bevel gears can change motor orientation and location, but add components, alignment requirements, losses, and potential maintenance points.
  * **Use a stop/go design gate.** If the fundamental geometry cannot satisfy the competition envelope and required articulation, redesigning the concept may be more effective than adding mechanical complexity to preserve it.

These are useful review prompts for PiWars chassis development. They are not a recommendation to use a belt or bevel-gear drive on another robot without a separate load, efficiency, tolerance, and serviceability assessment.

===== Relevance to the project =====

Reference for PiWars chassis packaging, tracked drivetrain layout, and rocker-bogie articulation. It may also inform general ROV mechanical packaging practice where moving assemblies, thrusters, guards, and service access compete for a constrained envelope, but the source itself concerns a land robot and does not demonstrate an underwater application.

No design has been adopted or validated on the project's robots as a result of this reference.
