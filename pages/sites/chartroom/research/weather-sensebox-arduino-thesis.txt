===== Summary =====

Jan Alexander Wirwahn's 2012 bachelor's thesis describes an Arduino-based weather station intended for school projects. The prototype combines an Arduino Mega 2560 and Ethernet Shield with microSD logging, a real-time clock, temperature and humidity, pressure, rain, wind-speed, and wind-direction sensors. It is designed as a modular platform that can be extended with more sensors.

Temperature, humidity, and pressure are sampled every 10 seconds and averaged over 10 minutes before timestamped comma-separated-value (CSV) records are written to the SD card. Wind direction is sampled and stored at a shorter interval because the analogue vane output varied enough that longer averaging could produce misleading directions. This is a useful example of recording data locally before adding a network service.

===== Reported comparison and limitations =====

The thesis reports two comparison sessions totalling about 50 hours and more than 300 measurement series against a professional station. The author adjusted the temperature and pressure readings after the first session. The humidity readings failed above about 70% relative humidity. Wind speed was lower than the reference station; the author notes the prototype's anemometer was at about 2 m while the reference was at 5 m, with a rooftop obstacle also potentially affecting exposure. Wind direction varied substantially, and the author identified placement as a possible cause. The thesis therefore illustrates commissioning and comparison problems; it does not establish long-term accuracy of the prototype.

The hardware is now historical: its listed sensors include SHT15 and BMP085 devices. The thesis's educational platform and data-quality lessons remain useful, but its parts, libraries, and wiring should not be treated as current build instructions.

===== Relevance =====

The example supports separating sensor acquisition and timestamped local logging from later data sharing. It also shows why reference comparisons need similar sensor exposure and why a plausible sensor output does not establish representative measurements. These are research lessons for weather-station and buoy planning, not evidence of an implemented Robots project system.
