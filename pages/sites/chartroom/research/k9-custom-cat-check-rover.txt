===== Source =====

  * Video demonstration: [[https://youtu.be/7d6gPzREK-k|Custom cat-check rover — YouTube]]
  * Author's accompanying description was supplied in the research discussion. The author describes a year-long custom build intended to let them check on and interact with their cats while away.
  * Source claims and specifications below are attributed to the author; they have not been independently verified from schematics, source code, measurements, or a full teardown.

===== Author-reported system =====

The author describes the following implementation:

  * **Compute:** Raspberry Pi 5.
  * **Imaging:** Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 Wide.
  * **Drive and camera gimbal:** brushless DC (BLDC) motors.
  * **Battery:** 128 Wh LiFePO₄ battery.
  * **Power management:** custom power-management electronics and low-power sleep/standby modes, intended to extend unattended operating time.
  * **Electronics:** custom-designed PCBs.
  * **Mechanical construction:** resin 3D-printed enclosure/casing.
  * **Remote access and control:** custom iOS application and Tailscale, described as providing low-latency remote teleoperation over a private network without routing video through a commercial pet-camera cloud service.

The author characterises the rover as functional but not fully polished. The stated objective is remote observation and interaction with pets while travelling.

===== Relevance to K9 =====

This is a useful systems-integration reference rather than a design to copy wholesale.

  * **Onboard compute and camera:** Raspberry Pi 5 and Camera Module 3 Wide provide a concrete reference for combining mobile robotics compute with remote visual feedback. Consider camera field of view, mounting, lighting, video latency, thermal load, and camera access from the operator interface.
  * **Teleoperation and networking:** Tailscale is worth investigating as a secure remote-access overlay for reaching a rover on a private network. Assess peer-to-peer connectivity, relay behaviour when direct paths are unavailable, authentication, device-key lifecycle, network availability, and recovery after disconnection. Do not assume that a private overlay alone provides application-level safety or deterministic control.
  * **Control separation:** For K9, retain the intended separation between high-level Raspberry Pi functions and a dedicated low-level motor controller. Network and application latency must not be part of the wheel-speed control loop.
  * **BLDC drive:** The author's use of BLDC motors is a comparison point only. K9's current investigation concerns two RS550 brushed DC geared motors with independent encoder-feedback speed loops. A change to BLDC would require a separate assessment of motor controllers, commutation, feedback, braking, current limits, and control interfaces.
  * **Camera gimbal:** The reported motorised gimbal may provide ideas for camera positioning, but the actuator type, feedback, stabilisation method, and control implementation are not stated in the supplied description.
  * **Power management:** The 128 Wh LiFePO₄ pack and low-power standby approach are worth examining for energy budgeting, controlled shutdown, wake-up strategy, and standby current. The battery chemistry and capacity are not a direct recommendation for K9; K9's current battery investigation is a 3S2P 21700 lithium-ion pack.
  * **Custom PCBs and enclosure:** The project is an example of integrated custom electronics and a resin-printed enclosure. Inspect PCB partitioning, serviceability, thermal management, connector strategy, ingress protection, and repairability before drawing conclusions.

===== Questions for further investigation =====

  - [ ] Inspect the video for chassis layout, wheel arrangement, suspension, motor mounting, and camera placement.
  - [ ] Establish whether the BLDC drive motors have integrated encoders or other speed/position feedback.
  - [ ] Determine whether the camera gimbal is independently controlled and whether it has position feedback or stabilisation.
  - [ ] Establish the software architecture between the iOS application, Tailscale connection, Raspberry Pi application, and motor-control hardware.
  - [ ] Determine how video is transported, what latency is achieved, and whether the author uses direct peer-to-peer paths or Tailscale DERP relays in typical operation.
  - [ ] Identify the behaviour on loss of network, stale commands, application crash, or Pi reboot; confirm that propulsion defaults to a safe state locally.
  - [ ] Investigate the power-management circuit, standby current, wake-up mechanism, battery protection, and shutdown sequencing.
  - [ ] Look for published PCB design files, firmware, application code, and measured runtime data.

===== Limitations =====

The available source material for this record is the author's supplied description and linked video. The following are not established by that description alone: detailed chassis dimensions or materials beyond the reported resin-printed casing; BLDC motor type and ratings; motor feedback architecture; motor-controller design; gimbal mechanism; battery cell configuration and BMS; measured runtime; power-saving current; video codec, bitrate, and latency; network topology under real-world conditions; and the safety response to communications failure.

Do not treat the author's performance descriptions (including “low latency”, “silent”, or “extreme power-saving”) as measured or independently validated results.
