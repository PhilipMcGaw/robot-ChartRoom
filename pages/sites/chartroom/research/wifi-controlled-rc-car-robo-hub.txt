===== Scope =====

This note records the build described by Hackaday as a simple smartphone-controlled car from ROBO HUB. Hackaday links to an Instructables guide titled “Make 3d printed Smartphone Controlled Wi-Fi Car”. The Instructables page did not expose readable content during review, so this summary relies on Hackaday's description and does not verify the original build steps or files.

===== Reported design =====

Hackaday describes a NodeMCU ESP8266 microcontroller, an L293D motor driver, and three 18650 lithium-ion cells. The ESP8266 drives brushed direct-current motors in a differential-drive arrangement, with the motors on each side paired for tank-style steering. A smartphone app provides the control interface. The chassis is described as a four-wheel, 3D-printed build.

The article's description and reader comments leave some mechanical details unclear: Hackaday calls out geared motors on the front wheels and simple brushed motors on the rear, while a comment says the front motors are not connected and serve as wheel mounts. Treat the drivetrain details as unresolved until the original guide or build files can be inspected.

===== Relevance =====

This is a low-cost educational reference for Wi-Fi teleoperation, differential drive, and the division between a wireless microcontroller and a motor driver. It may be useful when comparing simple hobby-car control interfaces, but it provides no evidence of command latency, link-loss behaviour, electrical protection, or safe operation around people.
