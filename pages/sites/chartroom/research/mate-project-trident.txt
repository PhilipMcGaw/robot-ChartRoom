===== Scope =====

Project Trident is a student-led team at The Athenian School in California. Its website describes its 2026 MATE ROV entry, TRI26, and a separate open-source ROV effort called OpenBot. This note records the team's public overview; it is not an independent evaluation of the design or performance claims.

===== What the team describes =====

The team describes TRI26 as an eight-thruster vectored ROV with six-degree-of-freedom manoeuvring, a three-camera system, an articulating claw, photogrammetry, computer vision, augmented-reality control, and a modular electronics enclosure. It also presents its Mission Management Terminal (MMT) as a crew-coordination interface that tracks mission tasks, time, and practice-run results for the pilot and other operators.

The same site describes OpenBot as a separate open-source ROV for marine photogrammetry and future modification. The site also lists proposed TRI27 features as early development with details pending; those items should not be read as implemented features.

===== Relevance =====

The vehicle overview may be useful for considering mission support beyond piloting: shared crew state, task sequencing, run logging, and operator displays. The site's distinction between TRI26 and OpenBot is also useful when checking whether a claim applies to the competition vehicle or the separate open-source project.

===== Limitations =====

The source is the team's own promotional and project overview, not a detailed engineering report or published codebase. Its performance, depth, cost, and feature statements are team claims. Treat planned or early-development items as proposals unless supported by released design files, software, or test evidence.
