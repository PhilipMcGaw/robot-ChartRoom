===== What the source reports =====

K&J Magnetics describes three generator iterations using permanent magnets, wound coils, and 3D-printed parts. The first design used a diametrically magnetised ring magnet and a three-coil radial-flux stator, with about 250 turns per coil. The company says this produced less than 1 V when spun with a battery-powered drill. A six-coil version increased the voltage, but still did not light the example LED and required the drill at maximum speed.

The final experiment used eight stepped block magnets and 12 coils, each with about 230 turns. The coils were connected in three Wye-connected groups, with four coils in series in each group, and the output was rectified with diodes. The article reports 4–5 V when the generator was turned by hand and 20 V when driven by a fast drill. It describes the output as under 1 W.

===== Relevance and limits =====

This is a useful educational reference for how magnet count, coil turns, coil arrangement, and rectification affect a small permanent-magnet generator. It may inform early energy-harvesting experiments for sensor nodes, but the article does not report a wind-speed test, rotational-speed measurements, a load curve, or sustained energy output. The reported drill-driven voltage is not evidence of practical wind-turbine performance or suitability as a field power source.
