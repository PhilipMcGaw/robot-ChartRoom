===== Sources =====

  * Reddit discussion: [[https://www.reddit.com/r/IOT/s/8IL6NicXs3|Four and a half years of cellular bus signs from scratch: what worked, what didn't, and how I ended up in IoT]]

===== Summary =====

The case study describes three solar-powered, cellular-connected real-time bus departure signs operated over approximately four and a half years. Its value is the operational experience: power consumption, firmware recovery, diagnostics, and the cost and complexity of maintaining devices in the field.

==== Lessons worth reviewing ====

  - **Optimise the load before increasing generation.** The Reddit summary reports that display panels drew approximately 2.8 W even with their LEDs off. Reducing quiescent consumption was more effective than simply fitting a larger solar panel. For battery-powered Robot Code nodes, measure sleep current, regulator losses, wake-up transients, and radio transmit energy before sizing the energy source.
  - **Treat recovery logic as a state machine.** A battery fault and repeated resets interacted with a cellular modem's network-attachment restriction, making recovery harder. Avoid unbounded retries and reboot loops; distinguish sensor, radio, power, and firmware faults, and use bounded retries with back-off.
  - **Keep diagnostics across resets.** A persistent fault caused repeated watchdog resets, while logs were erased on each reboot. Preserve reset-cause flags, boot counters, and a compact last-fault record; retain telemetry until it can be sent.
  - **Make remote observability part of the initial design.** The signs initially lacked useful remote visibility. A custom telemetry and device-management platform was later developed to avoid unsuitable commercial polling costs and dependencies. For Robot Code, a simple last-seen indicator, stale-data alert, and diagnostic telemetry could provide much of the immediate value.
  - **Validate the whole deployed system.** Test power budgets, message frequency and payload sizes, storage growth, reconnect behaviour, and failure recovery under realistic conditions—not only during a successful bench demonstration.

===== Relevance to Robot Code =====

  * **LoRa weather station and buoy:** favour low-power operation, scheduled sampling and transmission, and measurement-led battery sizing. Rain detection can wake a node when needed; temperature nodes can sample and transmit periodically.
  * **PHP/MySQL telemetry backend:** the experience supports keeping the data path and stored telemetry under project control rather than taking on a proprietary cloud dependency without a clear benefit.
  * **Gateway and embedded nodes:** include bounded retry/back-off behaviour, reset-cause reporting, uptime, battery voltage, and radio-health diagnostics where the hardware supports them.
  * **ROV systems:** persistent fault records and useful remote diagnostics are transferable lessons, although the bus sign's cellular-specific behaviour is not directly applicable to an ROV.

===== Important caveat =====

The bus signs used cellular connectivity and frequent polling of an external real-time data service. Robot Code's LoRa nodes have a different communications and power profile. Cellular data costs, network attachment restrictions, and modem-specific recovery behaviour must not be assumed to apply to LoRa.

===== Review questions =====

  * What are the measured sleep, idle, sampling, and transmit currents for each node?
  * Which fault and reset details should survive power loss or watchdog reset?
  * What is the retry/back-off policy for sensor reads, LoRa transmission, and gateway/backend delivery?
  * How will the backend distinguish a valid unchanged reading from a node that has stopped reporting?
  * Which lessons from the PidgeIoT implementation are applicable, and what hardware/software trade-offs does its detailed story reveal?
