===== ROV project overview =====

Hamish Trolove's [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/OpenSource_ROV.html|OpenSource ROV]] archive describes a home-built remotely operated vehicle (ROV) with a topside Arduino controller and an onboard Arduino connected over a tether. The control pages describe a 100 m Category 5 (CAT5) cable, serial communications, and later use of the EasyTransfer library. A PlayStation 2 controller provides operator inputs. The system controls thrusters, lights, and camera functions, and returns vehicle readings for display. The fourth control-sketch edition adds depth and heading sensors; the author identifies it as the latest edition and links the first three as superseded versions.

The archive also includes an idle sketch intended to hold the vehicle outputs in a neutral state during development, experiments with joystick and Arduino-to-Arduino communications, electronic speed controller (ESC) calibration sketches, and a pressure and temperature logger used during pressure testing. These pages describe a particular legacy build and its development. Their component choices, pin assignments, libraries, and procedures are not current project requirements.

The project-update and trial pages provide a useful chronology, but the results are the builder's own reports. The bath article first describes a buoyancy check without electronics installed, then a powered trial. A later pool trial reports tether drag and an ESC that cut out; the builder attributed the cut-out to ESC overheating, and later project material describes a change to a different controller model. In a June 2016 update, the builder reports that a pressure-chamber test of the complete electronics pod found leaks. Separately, the wire-seal articles report that test samples held a 5 bar pressure difference for at least 30 minutes. These are different test articles and outcomes: the sample result does not show that the assembled pod passed its pressure test.

===== Design and use limits =====

The project's thruster, tethered-video, seal, and shell-analysis pages are useful as examples of design questions and build documentation. The shell page presents analytical calculations and software estimates for its own pipe and end caps; those estimates are not independent validation, and must not be used as a depth rating or design basis for another pressure housing. Seal geometry, materials, manufacturing tolerances, penetrators, and pressure tests must be assessed for the actual design.

The ESC calibration pages identify different sequences for different controller types, and one ROV calibration page is explicitly superseded. Do not apply a sequence, signal range, or wiring diagram without checking the exact ESC manufacturer's instructions and the actual system. The Arduino serial examples demonstrate project-specific messages and links; they do not define a robust protocol or establish that ordinary logic-level serial is suitable for a long tether. The inter-integrated circuit (I²C) page itself warns that it is for short wiring. The propeller article describes OpenSCAD models developed for a radio-controlled aircraft, not a validated underwater propeller design.

The articles are independent references, not evidence that any of their designs or software has been tested or adopted in the Robots project. Several pages link downloadable sketches, models, spreadsheets, or drawings; those files were not reviewed or copied into Chartroom.

===== Educational robotics and small circuits =====

The linked teaching materials use older mBot, Scratch/mBlock, and Lego Mindstorms EV3 contexts. They may help with introductory activities on sensing, control logic, and chassis design, but their software interfaces, hardware availability, and examples should be checked before reuse.

  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics1a-Introduction_to_the_mBot.html|Robotics 1a – Introduction to the mBot]] — mBot hardware, sensors, and programming modes.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics1b-Building_an_mBot_Programme.html|Robotics 1b – Building an mBot Program]] — connecting, programming, and uploading a basic mBot programme.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics1c-Programming_a_Guided_mBot.html|Robotics 1c – Programming a Guided mBot]] — timed movement instructions for a guided maze route.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics1e-More_mBot_Sensors.html|Robotics 1e – More mBot Sensors]] — line-following sensor exercises.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics1f-mBot_Sensor_lucky-Dip.html|Robotics 1f – mBot Sensor Lucky-Dip]] — open-ended input/output activities; the page states a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics2a-Introduction_to_the_Mindstorm_EV3s.html|Robotics 2a – Introduction to the Lego Mindstorm EV3s]] — EV3 parts and chassis design process.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics2b-Mindstorm_EV3_Body_Building.html|Robotics 2b – Lego Mindstorms EV3 Body Building]] — chassis design and build checks.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics2c-Mindstorm_EV3_Programming_Interface.html|Robotics 2c – Lego Mindstorms EV3 Programming Interface]] — graphical motor, loop, and logic blocks.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Robotics2d-Mindstorm_EV3_Chassis_Action.html|Robotics 2d – Lego Mindstorms EV3 Chassis in Action]] — sensor-based chassis challenge.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/Flex_Sensor_and_Servo.html|Flex Sensor and Servo]] — analogue input and servo example with a start/stop control.
  * [[https://www.techmonkeybusiness.com/articles/MCP121_BEAMbot.html|MCP121 Based Solar Engine BEAMbot]] — discrete voltage-supervisor circuit for a solar-powered motor; the author labels the explanation as tentative.
