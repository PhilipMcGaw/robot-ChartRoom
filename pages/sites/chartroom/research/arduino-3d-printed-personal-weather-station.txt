===== Reported design =====

Arduino Blog's 2018 article describes a local weather-measurement station made for an arts and science exhibition. It combines a 3D-printed rain gauge, anemometer, and weather vane with a barometer and temperature sensor. The sensors connect to an Arduino Uno, and readings appear on a 4×20-character liquid-crystal display (LCD).

The article explicitly says the demonstration needs calibration for real-world use. The quoted creator notes say the rainfall and wind-speed instruments need calibration, while temperature and barometric pressure do not. The weather vane uses eight reed switches and a magnet to encode sixteen directions; the quoted notes describe positioning the magnet so it can trigger adjacent switches between direction positions.

===== Relevance and limits =====

This is a reference for combining printed mechanical sensor parts with simple embedded electronics and a local display. The rain and wind mechanisms may be useful when comparing sensor-node designs, but their calibration and long-term outdoor performance are not established here. The Arduino Blog post does not describe a remote telemetry link or network service.

The article links to an original Thingiverse model page, but that page returned HTTP 403 during this review. The design files, electronics schematic, firmware, and licence therefore remain unreviewed; use the original model page for those details if access becomes available.
