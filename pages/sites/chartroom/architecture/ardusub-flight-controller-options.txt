===== Decision summary =====

For a Raspberry Pi 4 running BlueOS, the **Blue Robotics Navigator** is the most direct fit. It is designed to turn the Raspberry Pi 4 into a BlueOS/ArduSub flight controller and provides the onboard IMU, compass, barometer, PWM outputs, ADC inputs, leak inputs, I²C, and serial expansion needed by the vehicle.

A conventional alternative is a **Cube Orange+** or **Holybro Pixhawk 4** running ArduSub, with the Raspberry Pi acting as the BlueOS companion computer. This keeps the autopilot control loop on a dedicated controller and is the lower-risk architecture for a vehicle intended to operate in water.

===== Recommendation for the CuttleOS/ChartRoom ROV =====

==== Navigator ====

Choose the Navigator if the goal is to use the Raspberry Pi as the main onboard computer while retaining the normal BlueOS and ArduSub workflow. It also leaves the Navigator's expansion I²C and serial interfaces available for additional sensors.

The Navigator is the closest match to the proposed design, but the external IMU and Modbus depth sensor should not automatically replace its onboard control-state sensors. Any sensor used for stabilization or depth-hold needs to be integrated into ArduPilot's supported sensor path and validated under failure conditions.

Blue Robotics' [[https://bluerobotics.com/learn/navigator-hardware-setup/#telemetry-radio|Navigator Hardware Setup]] guide says a telemetry radio can connect to any of the six-pin serial connectors and recommends SERIAL 1 for this purpose. Check the radio's supply and signal requirements against the Navigator connector specification before wiring it.

For other serial devices, Blue Robotics documents the six-pin SERIAL 1, 3, 4, and 5 connectors, and says additional devices can connect through a Raspberry Pi 4 USB port with a USB-to-UART adapter. The published pinout for the SERIAL 1, 3, and 4 connectors identifies a 5 V supply pin, 3.3 V TX/RX signals, and ground. Match the device supply and logic levels to the connector specification; do not treat the 5 V supply pin as a 5 V serial signal. The guide lists the Linux device paths as ''/dev/ttyS0'' for SERIAL 1, ''/dev/ttyAMA1'' for SERIAL 3, ''/dev/ttyAMA2'' for SERIAL 4, and ''/dev/ttyAMA3'' for the UART on SERIAL 5 + I²C 6.

==== Cube Orange+ or Pixhawk 4 ====

Choose a Cube Orange+ or Pixhawk 4 when reliability and a conventional separation of responsibilities matter more than reducing board count:

<code text>
Raspberry Pi 4
    └── BlueOS, video, networking, extensions
            │ MAVLink / USB or UART
            ▼
Dedicated autopilot
    └── ArduSub, IMU, attitude/depth control, PWM outputs
</code>

This is the architecture most closely aligned with the established ArduSub/BlueOS ecosystem.

==== Pixhawk 6C ====

The Pixhawk 6C is a potentially good value modern ArduPilot board. However, Blue Robotics' specific ArduSub guidance recommends Navigator, Pixhawk 4, and Cube hardware; other ArduPilot-compatible boards are not necessarily tested with BlueOS. Treat the 6C as a test-and-integration choice rather than the lowest-risk purchase.

===== Hardware checklist =====

Before ordering, confirm the complete system rather than the controller price alone:

  * Raspberry Pi 4B and BlueOS-compatible storage;
  * autopilot power module or suitable regulated power input;
  * PWM/ESC cables and connector compatibility;
  * underwater pressure/depth sensor and its electrical interface;
  * USB-RS485 adapter or RS485 transceiver for Modbus sensors;
  * leak detection and hardware motor-disable path;
  * enclosure, vibration isolation, and power distribution;
  * ArduSub firmware support for the exact board and selected sensor drivers.

===== Connection diagram reference =====

The [[https://www.ardusub.com/introduction/hardware-options/connection-diagrams.html|ArduSub connection diagrams]] page gives a system-level view of the autopilot, companion computer, topside computer, tether, power, thrusters, and optional peripherals. It is useful as a conceptual reference when discussing how these components relate.

The page carries an archive notice and says that the documentation is no longer maintained. Do not use it as the current authority for wiring, voltage limits, or hardware compatibility. The notice points readers to the [[https://ardupilot.org/sub/|ArduPilot Sub documentation]], which is being updated from the original ArduSub documentation.

The archived [[https://www.ardusub.com/introduction/hardware-options/required-hardware.html|Required Hardware]] page gives a system-level checklist that includes an autopilot, companion and topside computers, joystick, camera, electronic speed controls, thrusters, power sensing and distribution, and a tether. Treat this as historical system context, not a current bill of materials or confirmation that each item is required for this vehicle.

The user-provided [[https://www.ardusub.com/introduction/required-software/ardusub-autopilot-firmware.html|ArduSub Autopilot Firmware]] page is also archived. It explains the distinction between ArduSub firmware and other ArduPilot vehicle firmware, but its version examples (Stable 4.0.x and Development 4.1.x) and its board-support statement are historical. Use the current [[https://ardupilot.org/sub/docs/common-loading-firmware.html|ArduPilot Sub firmware-loading guide]] and [[https://ardupilot.org/sub/docs/common-autopilots.html|autopilot hardware list]] for present-day firmware selection and board support. Compatibility must be checked for the exact autopilot; this reference does not establish that a particular board is supported or tested for this vehicle.
